07

Icon system

Thirteen modules · thirteen pictograms

One fixed pictogram per module, so a user recognises the module before reading its label. The icons follow the mark's own geometry, not an external library style.

Construction
Sharp corners only — no rounded joins, no rounded caps — matching the logo's construction principle in section 03. Monoline stroke at 1.5px. Built from straight lines and right angles wherever possible; a curve is permitted only where the pictogram is unreadable without one, and even then the terminals stay square.
Sizing
16px
20px
24px
No new size token. 16px in the collapsed icon rail and table rows. 20px in the expanded sidebar — dense enough that a smaller icon keeps the label leading, not competing. 24px on cards and other standalone placements, where the icon carries more visual weight on its own.
Colour
Existing tokens only. White on the active row, white at reduced opacity when inactive, Primary Indigo on light surfaces outside the sidebar. Never a second colour and never gold except where the icon sits inside a gold-background element — rare, and never in the sidebar. One named exception to the no-fill rule: the small midpoint square on Interface Management, which is filled by design to mark the point of contact between two parties. No other icon may use a fill, and no future icon may add one without amending this rule explicitly.

08

Colour meaning

What the colour represents, not only where it appears

Sections 01 through 07 specify where each token may appear. This section specifies what it means. The same meaning holds across website, LinkedIn, presentations, documents and product UI, so a reader who has seen one RTPM surface can read the next one faster.

RTPM Indigo #0D08D2
Insight, principle, conclusion, operating truth, key concept.
Indigo marks the thing that is true. It carries the argument: the definition, the principle being stated, the conclusion the reader should leave with. It is the default emphasis colour on light grounds.
RTPM Gold #FFCC00
Breakthrough, contrast, challenge, key takeaway.
Gold marks the turn in the argument: the point where the reader's assumption breaks, the takeaway, the single action requested. It appears on dark grounds only, and on the website only as a CTA.
09

Hierarchy doctrine

Communication is solved in this order. Each mechanism is exhausted before the next is used. Colour is reinforcement, never the primary mechanism.

Position> Size> Colour> Weight
Position
Where it sits on the canvas, reading order, and the whitespace around it. Most hierarchy problems are solved here.
Size
Scale contrast between the primary statement and its support. A large step reads as hierarchy; a small step reads as noise.
Colour
One accent per canvas, applied per section 10. Used to confirm what layout already established.
Weight
The last adjustment, and the smallest. If weight is doing the work, the layout above it has not been solved.
10

Highlight rule

Governs RTPM 2, RTPM 4 and every future surface
Dark ground
Responsibility without authority
Gold highlight. One contiguous span, one to four words, standing alone as a phrase.
Light ground
Responsibility without authority
Indigo highlight. Gold is never used as text on a light ground — it does not hold contrast and it reads as a warning colour.
Never both on one canvas. One accent colour per canvas, chosen by the ground it sits on. The website carries a further restriction: gold appears only as a CTA button on a dark ground, never as text emphasis. Indigo may be used sparingly there for category labels, proof points, statistics and section anchors.
Executive credibility test. If a canvas requires multiple highlights to communicate its message, the message is not yet clear enough. Rewrite the statement rather than adding a second highlight.
11

Signature structures

RTPM narrative patterns · repeat, do not reinvent

Six recurring oppositions carry the RTPM argument. They are treated as intellectual property: the same six, in the same wording, across marketing, presentations, website and sales material. Recognition comes from repetition, not from variation.

Chasing vs Real-Time
The cost of asking for status instead of seeing it.
Responsibility vs Authority
Accountability held without the means to act on it.
Manual vs Automated
Effort spent assembling the picture instead of acting on it.
Fragmented vs Single Source of Truth
One record everyone reads, rather than several that disagree.
Information vs Knowledge
Data present in the system versus understanding available to the decision.
Reporting vs Visibility
Status produced on request versus status that is simply there.
Master brand principle · tie-breaker
RTPM should resemble a board presentation before it resembles a marketing campaign.
Used whenever a visual decision is genuinely open. The palette does not expand, no accent colour is added, and no canvas gains a second highlight to resolve an ambiguity. Authority comes from consistency, restraint and hierarchy.
