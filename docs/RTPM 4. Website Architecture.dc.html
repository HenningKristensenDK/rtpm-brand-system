Website architecture

The last surface. A website for owner-side buyers evaluating a control platform, not a campaign site: role-based routes, sector-specific paths, and a procurement layer a CIO can read without asking for a call. Examples render at 1440px, true size — scroll each one sideways if your window is narrower. All copy is placeholder except the approved category line.

Type rule for this surface. The website runs on Inter only — the same as the product. Barlow Semi Condensed is a marketing voice and does not appear on rtpm.dk. A buyer who moves from the site into the app should not notice a change of typeface; that continuity is part of the trust argument. Text secondary #595B78 on the page tint #F7F7FB clears WCAG AA for body copy — see RTPM 1. Core Tokens, "Contrast requirements". Below 15px it is used for captions only, never for running body text.
Emphasis, not highlight
Word-level gold text highlighting — the Accent Gold rule in RTPM 1 and the carousel and headline highlight pattern in RTPM 2 — is a marketing-only device and does not apply to rtpm.dk. The website's emphasis mechanism is the category line set in Primary Indigo, small caps, on light surfaces, exactly as stated in the homepage hero rule in section A.
Gold on this site is reserved for exactly one CTA button per page, inside the one navy band described in Surface rhythm — never for headline words, and never on a light background, per the dark-only rule in RTPM 1. No website headline, on any page type, carries a bold-yellow word span. If a future page needs word-level emphasis in body copy it uses Primary Indigo, following the same one-span, no-function-words discipline as the gold pattern — different in colour only.
Category first, product second
The visitor must know what kind of thing RTPM is inside four seconds. Features come after the category is established, never before.
Routes, not a funnel
Five roles and two sectors reach different pages. A €500M data centre PMC and a renewables portfolio director do not share an argument.
Proof over promise
Real product screens, real workflow sequences, real governance answers. No stock photography, no abstract illustration, no invented metrics.
Light, with one dark band
Paper white throughout. Ink Navy appears once per page as a single full-bleed band, and it always carries the trust argument.

Role-based navigation

Five roles · five different arguments
The "By role" panel
Each role gets its own question, its own proof, and its own landing page.
Candidate for locking
Purpose
On a capital project the buying group is five people who disagree about what matters. An executive is asking about exposure, a CIO about tenancy, procurement about exit. One page cannot answer all five without becoming vague.
Must include
Five entries, each with the role name, the question that role actually asks, and a route. The panel is a directory, not a menu of features.
Layout rules
Full-width dropdown panel, five equal columns, opens on hover and on focus. Each column: role, question in quotes, three-line answer, one link. A right rail carries the sector split so a visitor can cross over.

No icons in this panel. Roles are not modules. The thirteen pictograms in RTPM 1. Core Tokens, section 07, are a module-only set and are never applied to a role, a sector or a page type.
What changes per role page — and what never does
Changes: the headline question, the product screens shown, the three outcomes listed, the proof format (portfolio table for executives, register for directors, ageing charts for PMO, architecture diagram for IT, terms for procurement), and the call to action's commitment level.
Never changes: the category line, the tenant statement, the header, the footer, the type scale, or the claim itself. Five entrances, one building — a director and a CIO comparing notes must recognise the same product.
✓Do
Write each role's question in their words, in quotes. It is the only copy they will read in the panel.
Give procurement and IT full pages, not a section of the FAQ. They are the two roles that stop deals.
Keep the sector rail in the panel so an unidentified visitor still has a route.
×Don't
List modules in the role panel. A role does not want a feature index.
Add a sixth role. Five is the buying group; more dilutes each argument.
Change the category line or the claim per role. That reads as a different product each time.

Sector routes

Renewables · data centres
Two sectors, two vocabularies
The same platform, described in the terms each sector already uses internally.
Candidate for locking
Purpose
Credibility is vocabulary. A renewables director thinks in COD protection and grid connection; a data centre PMC thinks in RFS dates, OFCI scope and commissioning gates. Using the wrong noun costs the meeting.
Must include
Two routes only. Each names the sector's own risk language, the roles it maps to, and the modules that carry the weight in that sector.
Layout rules
Two equal cards on a light ground, no visual hierarchy between them — RTPM does not signal a preferred sector. Each card: sector, the failure it prevents, four terms in that sector's language, one route.

Procurement credibility layer

The page that decides whether there is a deal
Answers before the call
Data residency, governance, AI approval, exit and portability — stated in public, in writing.
Candidate for locking
Purpose
A small vendor selling into a utility or a hyperscaler fails on procurement, not on product. Publishing the answers removes the reason to disqualify RTPM early, and signals that the questions were expected.
Must include
Four sections, each with a plain statement and a downloadable artefact: data residency, governance and access, AI human-in-the-loop approval, exit and portability. A named contact, not a form.
Layout rules
Document-like, not marketed: navy band at the top carrying the position, then a light two-column body of question and answer. Every answer is a sentence a buyer can paste into a tender response.

Surface rhythm

Three surfaces · no new token
A surface change signals a change of purpose
Not alternation. Stripes stop meaning anything by the third one.
Candidate for locking
Purpose
A long page needs orientation without navigation. Three surfaces give a reader landmarks: this section argues, this section shows, this section is the commitment. The rhythm does the work a subheading cannot.
Design principles
Three surfaces, all already locked in Step 1 — no colour is added for this. The tone change is the boundary, so no rule is drawn between sections. Never two tinted sections in a row: adjacent tints merge and the boundary disappears.
Mobile breakpoints
Hero stacks under 768pxThe 52/48 split becomes one column, text above the workflow sequence. Single-column stacking and the 16px gutter follow RTPM 3, section 09 — no separate mobile grid is defined here.
Role panel becomes an accordionHover and focus do not exist on a touch screen, so the five-column dropdown becomes a tap-based accordion, one role open at a time. Rows keep the 48px target from RTPM 3.
Sector cards stackThe two cards go vertical under 768px in the same order, neither promoted. Card radius, spacing and type floor are unchanged from the desktop rule.

✓Do
Change surface only where the section's job changes. Five changes on a long page is right.
Keep cards white on the tint so product screens lift off the ground.
Let the navy band carry the page's single gold action.
Open and close the body on white. Navy is a punctuation mark, not a chapter.
×Don't
Alternate white and tint section by section. That is a stripe, not a structure.
Place two tinted sections next to each other. They merge and the boundary is lost.
Draw a rule between two surfaces. The tone change is already the boundary.
Introduce a fourth surface, or tint a section a different colour to "break it up".

System complete. With Step 4 locked, all four surfaces of the brand system are specified: foundations and the mark, the product design language, the completion set, the marketing templates, and the website. Every page type on rtpm.dk is assembled from patterns already in the system — no new component was invented for this surface.
Three decisions for Henning
01 · Primary action wording
"Book a working session" instead of "Request a demo" — it promises a look at their own workflow, not a pitch. It also raises the commitment. Confirm, or name the wording you use in outreach today.
02 · Publishing exit terms
The governance page publishes exit and portability openly, ungated. That is unusual and it is the strongest signal on the site. Confirm you are willing to commit those terms in public.
03 · No pricing page
Commercial model is described in structure with no figures until the model is fixed. The alternative is a tier grid you would have to defend. Say which you want live at launch.
